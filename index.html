Research Interests

My research interests lie in nonlinear dynamical systems, chemical reaction network theory, and mathematical biology, including mathematical epidemiology. I am particularly interested in the interplay between network structure and the qualitative behavior of nonlinear differential equations, including stability, persistence and global dynamics. My goal is to understand how structural and geometric properties of networks determine their dynamical behavior and to develop general mathematical frameworks for analyzing nonlinear systems.

I am also interested in applying the approaches in nonlinear dynamical systems to biological systems, particularly endemic dynamics with different settings such as media-response and people movement between patches.

Current Work

Convex Geometric Criteria for Weighted Horn–Jackson Dissipation

This work studies the existence of positive diagonal weights that make weighted Horn–Jackson functions non-increasing in generalized mass-action systems. It establishes finite convex-geometric criteria under specified assumptions on reaction network structure. Future studying plans please see the discussion part in the preprint below.

[Preprint / DOI] Zenodo, 2026

Evolutionary Origin of dgcY in Halophilic Bacteria

This project investigates the evolutionary origin of dgcY in halophilic bacteria through comparative genomics and phylogenetic analysis. It examines the incongruence between DgcY and 16S rRNA phylogenies, the genomic context of the Doe gene cluster, and the possibility of horizontal gene transfer (HGT).
